Financing a home that is still being built means planning around a moving date. The buyer is approved at contract, but the loan closes at completion, sometimes months later. Along the way the rate lock, the buyer's documents and the appraisal all have to line up with the build schedule. Planning those milestones at contract is the simplest way to protect the closing.

Key takeaways

  • Approve the buyer thoroughly at contract.
  • Choose a rate-lock strategy that fits the realistic completion date.
  • Expect to update documents and credit before closing.
  • Plan for the appraisal and completion inspection.
  • Share schedule changes with the lender as they happen.

At contract

Start with a fully reviewed approval, a loan program that fits the price and incentives, and a clear answer on whether the buyer has to sell another home. This is also when the lender should explain rate-lock options for the expected build time.

During construction

Rate locks can be timed in different ways. A buyer may lock early for certainty, often at extra cost for a longer lock, or float and lock closer to completion. Joseph explains the trade-offs to each buyer so the decision is made deliberately, not by default.

Buyers should also be coached not to change jobs, open new credit or move large sums of money during the build without calling their lender first.

Approaching completion

  • The lender updates the buyer's income, assets and credit
  • The appraisal is completed, or updated if the home was appraised from plans
  • The final inspection or certificate of occupancy is obtained
  • The closing is scheduled with the title company

The construction-timeline checklist

  1. Share the expected completion date at contract, and update it monthly.
  2. Decide on a rate-lock approach with the buyer and lender.
  3. Schedule document updates before completion.
  4. Confirm appraisal timing and any completion requirements.
  5. Coordinate the walk-through, inspection and closing date together.
