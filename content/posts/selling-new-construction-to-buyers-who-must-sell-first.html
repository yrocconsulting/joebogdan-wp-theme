TL;DR

Move-up buyers who must sell first are common and workable. Whether they can sign without a contingency depends on their income, equity and reserves, so builders should find out at the first meeting.

Many of a builder's best prospects already own a home. Whether they can sign without selling first depends on three things: whether their income supports both housing payments, how much equity is in their current home, and how much cash they have in reserve. If they qualify with both payments, they can buy without a sale contingency. If not, options like accessing equity, a contingency with a realistic timeline, or matching the build schedule to their sale can still make the deal work.

Key takeaways

  • Find out at the first meeting whether the buyer needs to sell.
  • Qualifying with both payments is the cleanest path.
  • Equity in the current home can fund the next down payment, within Texas homestead rules.
  • A long build can give a buyer time to sell, if the timeline is planned.
  • Get the lender involved before the contract, not after.

Why do move-up buyers need a financing plan?

A move-up buyer's current home is both their biggest asset and their biggest risk to your contract, so the financing plan for it matters from day one.

What options do move-up buyers have?

Move-up buyers may qualify carrying both homes, use their current home's equity, time the sale to the build, or use a realistic sale contingency.

  • Qualify with both payments. If income, credit and reserves support carrying both homes for a period, the buyer can sign without a sale contingency.
  • Use existing equity. Equity in the current home may help fund the down payment. Texas rules for borrowing against a homestead affect timing and amounts, so plan early.
  • Time the sale to the build. On a longer build, the buyer may be able to list and sell their current home before completion.
  • Rent the current home. Some programs allow a portion of documented rental income to count, which can help the buyer qualify.
  • A sale contingency. Works best when the current home is priced to sell and the timeline is realistic.

What should your sales team ask move-up buyers?

Sales teams should ask whether the buyer must sell first, how much equity they have and whether a lender has reviewed both payments.

  1. Do you own a home now, and do you plan to sell it or keep it?
  2. Is it listed, under contract or not yet on the market?
  3. Have you talked with a lender about qualifying with both payments?
