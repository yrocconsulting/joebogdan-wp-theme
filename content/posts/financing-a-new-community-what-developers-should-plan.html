How buyers will finance homes in a new community affects nearly every part of a development: who can afford the homes, how appraisals support pricing, how fast homes sell and how quickly capital comes back. Developers who plan end-buyer financing before phase one, including loan programs, tax impact, incentives and whether to sell lots, finished homes or hold rentals, avoid the most expensive surprises.

Key takeaways

  • Match price points and product to the loan programs your expected buyers will use.
  • Plan for appraisals in the first phase, when there are few comparable sales.
  • MUD and PID taxes change buyers' payments and what they qualify for.
  • Decide early which homes or lots you will sell and which you may hold as rentals.
  • Budget incentives by phase, including options such as forward commitments.

Which buyers will finance your homes?

First-time buyers, move-up buyers, retirees and investors use different loan programs with different limits and requirements. Knowing who your buyers are tells you which programs matter, what loan amounts fit and how sensitive buyers will be to rates and taxes.

Why do first-phase appraisals matter?

Early homes in a community often have few comparable sales nearby. Appraisers may rely on sales from other communities, so pricing and concessions in the first phase set the benchmark for every phase that follows.

How do MUD and PID taxes affect buyers?

Special district taxes are part of the monthly payment and of what buyers qualify for. A community with significant MUD or PID taxes needs pricing and incentives that account for them, and buyers need clear disclosure early.

Sell lots, sell homes or hold rentals?

  • Lot sales: buyers can use lot loans or construction-to-permanent loans with approved builders
  • Finished homes: standard purchase financing, with incentives planned by phase
  • Rentals: DSCR loans can finance completed homes held as rentals, qualifying on rent
