A jumbo loan is a mortgage larger than the conforming loan limit set each year by the Federal Housing Finance Agency. Because these loans cannot be sold to Fannie Mae or Freddie Mac, lenders set their own standards, which usually means closer review of credit, cash reserves, asset documentation and the appraisal. Strong preparation can improve your options and make the process smoother.

Key takeaways

  • Jumbo means above the conforming limit, which FHFA updates every year.
  • Expect more documentation of income and assets, and higher reserve expectations.
  • How you structure the down payment and how much liquidity you keep are strategic choices.
  • High-value, unique and acreage properties can make appraisals more complex.

What counts as a jumbo loan?

Any loan amount above the conforming loan limit for the county is considered jumbo. The limit changes each year and is higher in some high-cost areas. You can find the current limits on the FHFA website, or Joseph can confirm whether your loan amount falls into jumbo territory.

How is qualifying for a jumbo loan different?

  • Credit - lenders typically look for strong credit histories.
  • Reserves - you will usually need several months of payments in savings or investments after closing.
  • Asset documentation - expect to document where your down payment and reserves come from, including brokerage and retirement accounts.
  • Income - complex income from business ownership, bonuses, commissions or investments needs careful presentation.
  • Appraisal - higher-value and unique homes can be harder to value, and some lenders require additional review.

Down payment vs. liquidity: a strategic choice

With large loans, how much you put down is a real decision. A larger down payment can reduce the loan amount, while keeping more cash or investments can protect flexibility for your business, other properties or opportunities. There is no single right answer. Ranches, acreage and second homes

North Texas buyers often look at properties with acreage, outbuildings or agricultural use. These features can affect which programs fit and how the property is appraised. Second homes have their own occupancy rules. Share the property details early so the financing plan matches the property.

How to prepare

  1. Organize two years of income documentation and recent statements for every account you plan to use.
  2. Avoid moving large sums between accounts without a paper trail.
  3. Decide how much liquidity you want to keep after closing.
  4. Get a pre-approval before you shop, especially for competitive listings.
