TL;DR

A one-time close means one approval and one closing for construction and your mortgage. A two-time close uses two loans and two closings, adding flexibility but also the risk of re-qualifying when the home is finished.

A one-time close finances construction and your permanent mortgage in a single loan with one closing. A two-time close uses a short-term construction loan first, then a separate mortgage that pays it off when the home is finished. One-time closes offer more certainty and fewer closing costs; two-time closes offer more flexibility on the final mortgage, at the price of qualifying and closing twice.

Key takeaways

  • One-time close: one approval, one closing, no re-qualifying at the end.
  • Two-time close: two approvals, two closings and two sets of costs.
  • With a two-time close, your final mortgage depends on your credit, income and the market when the home is finished.
  • With a one-time close, the permanent loan terms are settled up front, depending on the program.
  • The right choice depends on your timeline, your cash and how stable your income will be during the build.

How do the two options compare?

A one-time close has one approval and one closing; a two-time close has two of each, with more flexibility but the risk of re-qualifying at the end.

  • Closings: one-time close has one; two-time close has two.
  • Qualifying: one-time close qualifies you once; two-time close requires qualifying again at completion.
  • Closing costs: one-time close has one set; two-time close usually has two.
  • Flexibility: two-time close lets you shop the permanent mortgage when the home is done.
  • Risk: with two-time close, a job change, credit change or market shift during construction can affect your final loan.

When does a one-time close make more sense?

A one-time close makes more sense when you value certainty, expect a long build or have income that could change during construction.

If you value certainty, expect a build of several months or longer, or have income that could change, a one-time close removes the risk of re-qualifying at the end. It also means one set of closing costs and less paperwork.

When can a two-time close make sense?

A two-time close can fit when you will sell another home during construction, your project does not fit a one-time program, or you want to shop permanent financing later.

A two-time close can fit when you expect to sell your current home during construction and put that money toward the final mortgage, when your project does not fit a one-time-close program, or when you want the freedom to choose different permanent financing later. Go in with a realistic plan for qualifying again.

What should you ask before choosing a construction loan structure?

Ask how long the build will really take, whether your income will stay steady, when you will sell another home and how much cash you want in reserve.

  • How long will construction realistically take, including weather and inspections?
  • Is my income likely to stay the same through the build?
  • Will I sell another home before or after the new one is finished?
  • How much cash do I want to keep in reserve for overruns and upgrades?
