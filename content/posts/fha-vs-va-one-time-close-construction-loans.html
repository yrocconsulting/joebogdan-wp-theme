Yes, you can build a new home with an FHA or VA loan. A one-time-close construction loan lets eligible buyers finance the lot, the construction and the permanent mortgage in a single FHA or VA loan with one closing. VA construction loans are for eligible veterans, service members and surviving spouses and carry no monthly mortgage insurance. FHA construction loans are open to any qualified buyer and offer more flexible credit guidelines. Both are for a home you will live in.

Key takeaways

  • FHA and VA one-time-close loans finance the lot, construction and permanent mortgage together.
  • Both are for primary residences only.
  • VA: for eligible veterans, service members and surviving spouses; no monthly mortgage insurance; a funding fee applies unless exempt.
  • FHA: flexible credit guidelines; FHA mortgage insurance and county loan limits apply.
  • Your builder must meet program requirements; VA requires a builder with a VA builder ID.

How do FHA and VA construction loans compare?

  • Who qualifies: FHA is open to qualified buyers; VA requires VA eligibility, confirmed by a Certificate of Eligibility.
  • Mortgage insurance: FHA has upfront and annual mortgage insurance; VA has no monthly mortgage insurance but a one-time funding fee, waived for some veterans.
  • Loan size: FHA loan limits are set by county; VA limits depend on your entitlement.
  • Occupancy: both require that you live in the home.
  • Builder: both require an eligible builder; VA requires a VA builder ID.

How does the process work?

  1. Confirm eligibility (and, for VA, your Certificate of Eligibility).
  2. Choose an eligible builder and finalize plans, specifications and budget.
  3. Appraise the home from plans, as if complete, to program standards.
  4. Close once, before construction begins, including the lot purchase if needed.
  5. Build with draws released after inspections; CrossCountry Mortgage offers interest-only payments during construction.
  6. Convert to a permanent 15- or 30-year FHA or VA mortgage once the home is complete.

Which one should you choose?

If you are VA-eligible, a VA construction loan is often worth comparing first because of its benefits, but it is not automatically the best fit. Funding fees, entitlement, and your builder's eligibility all matter. If you are not VA-eligible, FHA offers a path to build with flexible guidelines, and a conventional one-time close may fit buyers with stronger credit or a home that will not be their primary residence.
