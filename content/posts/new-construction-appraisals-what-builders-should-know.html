An appraisal on a new home tells the lender what the home is worth as security for the loan. For new construction, appraisers rely on recent sales of comparable homes, often in the same community, and they look closely at incentives and upgrades. If the appraisal comes in below the contract price, the buyer, builder and lender have to resolve the gap before closing, so it pays to understand how new-construction appraisals work.

Key takeaways

  • Appraisers rely heavily on recent comparable sales in and around the community.
  • Price cuts become comparables for future appraisals.
  • Upgrades add value only to the extent the market supports them.
  • Some appraisals are done from plans, with a completion inspection later.
  • A low appraisal can be addressed, but it is better to plan for it.

How appraisers value new homes

Appraisers compare the home with recent sales of similar homes, adjusting for size, lot, features and timing. In a new community, the builder's own recent closings are often the most relevant comparables. That is one reason pricing decisions, including price cuts, affect the value of every home that follows.

Upgrades and lot premiums

Design-center upgrades and lot premiums can support a higher price, but appraisers value them by what buyers in the market have paid, not by what they cost to build. Documenting upgrades clearly helps the appraiser understand what is in the home.

What if the appraisal comes in low?

  • Provide the appraiser with additional comparable sales or upgrade documentation through the lender's process
  • Renegotiate the price or restructure incentives
  • The buyer covers the difference with additional cash, if they are able

A buydown instead of a price cut can also help protect future comparables. See price cut or rate buydown.
