Builders have three main ways to spend incentive dollars on financing: a forward commitment that lowers the rate for many buyers at once, a buydown purchased for one buyer's loan, or a price cut. Forward commitments can stretch dollars furthest when you have predictable closings. Buydowns are the most flexible. Price cuts help buyers who need a lower loan amount, but they can lower your future comparable sales.

Key takeaways

  • Forward commitment: lower rate for many buyers, but you must predict volume.
  • Buydown: paid only when a specific home sells; flexible, but cost is per loan.
  • Price cut: simple and permanent, but affects appraisals for future homes.
  • All three can be limited by seller-concession rules and must be advertised carefully.

How do the three options compare?

  • Who benefits: forward commitment helps every eligible buyer in the window; buydowns and price cuts help one buyer at a time.
  • When you pay: forward commitments are paid up front; buydowns and price cuts are paid at closing.
  • Risk: forward commitments risk unused capacity; buydowns and price cuts do not.
  • Comparables: price cuts can lower future appraisals; rate incentives generally do not change the recorded price.
  • Buyer impact: rate incentives usually lower monthly payments more per dollar; price cuts lower the loan amount and cash to close.

How should a builder choose?

Start with your pipeline. If many homes will complete in the same window and buyers are payment-sensitive, a forward commitment may deliver the most. If closings are scattered or uncertain, buyer-specific buydowns keep you flexible. If buyers are struggling to qualify on loan amount or cash, a price cut or closing-cost help may fit better. Many builders use a mix.
