New-construction closings usually slip for predictable reasons: the buyer's documents went stale during the build, the rate lock expired, the final inspection or certificate of occupancy was late, the completion appraisal raised questions, or the title and lender teams were not coordinated with the build schedule. Most of these can be prevented by planning the financing around the construction timeline from the day the contract is signed.

Key takeaways

  • Long builds mean buyer documents and credit need updating before closing.
  • Rate locks have to be timed to realistic completion dates.
  • Lenders typically need the home complete, with final inspection, before closing.
  • A completion appraisal or final inspection can raise last-minute questions.
  • One coordinated schedule between builder, lender and title prevents most delays.

The five most common causes

  1. Stale documents. Pay stubs, bank statements and credit reports expire. If the file was approved months ago, it needs updating, and changes can surface late.
  2. Rate locks that run out. A lock that expires before completion can mean extension costs or a new rate.
  3. Completion paperwork. Lenders generally need the home finished, with the final inspection or certificate of occupancy in hand, before closing.
  4. Appraisal and completion questions. Unfinished items, value questions or missing documentation can hold up the final approval.
  5. Buyer changes during the build. A new car loan, a job change or a large deposit can change approval.

How to keep closings on schedule

  • Share realistic completion estimates with the lender and update them as the build moves.
  • Ask the lender to schedule document updates ahead of completion, not after.
  • Coach buyers not to open new credit, change jobs or move money without calling their lender.
  • Plan the rate lock around the build schedule, and discuss extension options up front.
  • Coordinate the final walk-through, inspection and closing date with the lender and title company in one shared timeline.
