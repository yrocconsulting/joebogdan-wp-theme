A strong pre-approval is based on documents that have actually been reviewed - income, assets and credit - not just numbers the buyer typed into a form. It is tailored to the offer, it comes from a loan officer the listing agent can reach, and it has no surprises waiting in underwriting. To sellers and their agents, that combination signals that the buyer can close.

Key takeaways

  • Pre-qualification is an estimate; pre-approval is based on verified information.
  • Letters should match the offer price and loan type.
  • Listing agents value a loan officer who answers questions quickly and clearly.
  • Issues found before the offer are much easier to solve than issues found after.

Pre-qualification vs. pre-approval

A pre-qualification is a quick estimate based on what you tell the lender. A pre-approval means your credit has been reviewed and your income and asset documents have been checked. A pre-approval takes a little more effort up front, and it is what serious sellers expect.

What sellers and listing agents look for

  • A letter from a lender they recognize or can easily verify
  • A loan amount and program that match the offer
  • Confidence that income and assets were actually reviewed
  • A loan officer who will pick up the phone and explain the file

For Realtors: what to ask your buyer’s lender

  1. Has the buyer’s income and asset documentation been reviewed, or only stated?
  2. Is there anything in the file that could cause a delay, such as a recent job change, large deposits or a home to sell?
  3. Will you call the listing agent if it helps the offer?
  4. How will you keep me updated through closing?

What buyers can do to keep a pre-approval strong

  • Avoid new credit accounts or large purchases before closing.
  • Do not change jobs without talking to your loan officer first.
  • Document large deposits and keep funds in the same accounts.
  • Respond quickly when the lender asks for updated documents.
