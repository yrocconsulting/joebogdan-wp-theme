TL;DR

Builder sales teams should expect fast responses on new buyers, clear answers about who can close, proactive updates on every file and closings coordinated with construction. Ask any lender how they deliver each one.

A builder's sales team should expect a lender to respond quickly to new buyers, give clear answers about whether a buyer can close, explain how incentives fit each loan program, plan financing around the construction schedule, and send proactive updates through closing. Just as important, a good lender protects your relationship with the buyer and never surprises your team at the closing table.

Key takeaways

  • Responsiveness to walk-in and model-home traffic matters most.
  • Clear, early answers about each buyer's real ability to close.
  • Incentive guidance that fits program limits.
  • Financing planned around construction milestones.
  • Regular status updates without your team having to chase them.

What should a lender do before the contract?

Before the contract, a lender should review the buyer's documents, confirm the right program and tell the builder plainly whether the buyer can close.

  • Prompt follow-up with buyers your team introduces
  • A straight answer on whether the buyer can close, and what it will take
  • Guidance on which incentive works best for that buyer's program

What should a lender do during the build?

During construction, a lender should update documents on schedule, plan the rate lock and send the builder proactive status updates.

  • A rate-lock plan tied to realistic completion dates
  • Document updates scheduled ahead of completion
  • Early warning if anything in the buyer's file changes

What should a lender do at completion?

At completion, a lender should clear final conditions quickly and coordinate the closing date with the builder and title company.

  • Coordination of the final inspection, appraisal and closing date
  • One point of contact for your team, the buyer and the title company
  • No surprises at the closing table

What should builders ask any lender they are considering?

Ask how quickly they respond, how they update your team, how they handle long builds and whether they have worked with builders before.

  1. How quickly do you follow up with a new buyer?
  2. How will you keep my team updated, and how often?
  3. How do you handle rate locks on longer builds?
  4. How do you handle buyers who are self-employed or need to sell first?
  5. Who is my contact if you are unavailable?
