A spec home is ready or nearly ready, so every week it sits under contract with a buyer who cannot close costs you carrying costs and lost buyers. The way to protect it is to verify the buyer before the contract is final: a reviewed pre-approval, a loan program that fits the home and your incentives, a clear answer on whether the buyer must sell first, and a lender who can close on your completion date.

Key takeaways

  • Spec homes have short timelines, so the buyer's financing must be ready now.
  • Verify the pre-approval, the program and any home the buyer must sell.
  • Match incentives to the buyer's program limits before signing.
  • Short option periods only help if the financing is already solid.
  • A responsive lender is part of protecting the inventory.

Why spec homes are different

With a to-be-built home, a financing problem can surface months before completion. With a spec home, the timeline is compressed. There is little room to fix documentation problems, change programs or wait for another home to sell, so the buyer's financing needs to be verified up front.

A spec-home buyer checklist

  1. A pre-approval based on reviewed income, assets and credit
  2. A loan program that fits the price, the home and your incentives
  3. Confirmation of whether the buyer must sell or rent another home
  4. Cash to close confirmed, including any amounts not covered by incentives
  5. A closing date the lender has confirmed it can meet

Marketing spec homes with financing

Financing incentives can help spec homes sell, but advertising a specific rate or payment triggers federal disclosure requirements. Promote the incentive generally, such as builder-paid closing costs or rate buydown options, and have your lender review any rate-based advertising before it runs.
