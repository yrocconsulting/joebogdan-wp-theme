A one-time-close construction loan, also called a construction-to-permanent loan, is a single loan that pays for building a new home and then converts into a regular mortgage when the home is complete. You apply, qualify and close once, before construction starts. During the build, the lender releases money to your builder in stages. When the home is finished, the loan becomes your permanent mortgage without a second closing.

Key takeaways

  • One application, one approval and one closing, before construction begins.
  • The lot purchase can often be included, and equity in land you own may count toward the down payment.
  • The builder is paid in draws after inspections confirm each stage of work.
  • Your builder must be reviewed and approved by the lender.
  • The home is appraised from plans and specifications, as if it were complete.

How does a one-time-close construction loan work?

  1. Plan the project. You, your builder and your loan officer agree on the budget, plans, lot and timeline.
  2. Approve the builder. The lender reviews the builder's experience, insurance, references and contract.
  3. Appraise from plans. An appraiser values the home as if complete, based on the plans, specifications and lot.
  4. Close once. You sign before construction starts. If you are buying the lot, it is usually purchased at this closing.
  5. Build with draws. As each stage is finished and inspected, funds are released to the builder.
  6. Convert. After the final inspection, the loan becomes your permanent mortgage.

What do you pay during construction?

Payments during the build are typically based on the amount that has been drawn so far, so they start small and grow as more of the home is built. Once the loan converts, regular principal-and-interest payments begin. How payments work during construction varies by program, so ask your loan officer to walk through it before you sign.

Who is a good fit for a one-time close?

  • Buyers building a custom home on their own lot or a lot they are buying
  • Buyers who want certainty about their permanent financing before construction starts
  • Buyers who would rather not pay for and qualify for two separate closings
  • Buyers whose income might change during a long build, since they will not need to re-qualify at the end

What can complicate a construction loan?

The most common problems are an unrealistic budget, a builder who is not ready for lender review, plans that change after closing, and construction that runs past the program's time limit. A contingency in the budget, an experienced builder and early planning prevent most of them. Texas also has specific requirements for construction contracts and liens, especially on a homestead, which your lender and title company will handle.
