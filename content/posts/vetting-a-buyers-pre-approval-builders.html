TL;DR

Not every pre-approval letter means the same thing. Before accepting one, builders should confirm that income, assets and credit were actually reviewed and that the approval fits the price, incentives and buyer's situation.

A pre-approval letter can mean anything from a fully reviewed file to a quick estimate based on what the buyer said. Before you take a home off the market, your sales team should know whether the buyer's income, assets and credit were actually reviewed, whether the loan program fits your contract and your timeline, and whether the buyer has to sell another home first. Seven questions to the buyer's lender will tell you most of what you need.

Key takeaways

  • Ask whether the lender reviewed documents or relied on what the buyer reported.
  • Confirm the loan program and that it allows your incentives and timeline.
  • Find out early if the buyer must sell another home.
  • For longer builds, ask how the lender will handle rate locks and re-verification.
  • A lender who answers clearly and quickly is a good sign in itself.

What seven questions should builders ask about a pre-approval?

Ask whether income, assets and credit were reviewed, which program applies, whether it covers your price and incentives, and whether the buyer must sell another home.

  1. Were income, assets and credit actually reviewed? Ask whether pay stubs, tax returns, bank statements and a credit report were reviewed, or only stated.
  2. Which loan program is the buyer approved for? Conventional, FHA, VA, jumbo and Non-QM loans have different rules for incentives, appraisals and timing.
  3. Does the approval cover this price and these incentives? A letter for a lower price, or one that ignores contribution limits, can unravel later.
  4. Does the buyer need to sell or rent another home? If so, ask whether they qualify carrying both payments.
  5. Is anything unusual in the file? Recent job changes, self-employment, large deposits or a credit event are not deal-breakers, but they need a plan.
  6. How will the loan work on our construction timeline? Ask about rate-lock options and what will need to be updated before closing.
  7. How will you keep our team updated? You want a lender who calls before problems become delays.

What are the red flags in a buyer's pre-approval?

Watch for letters with no documents reviewed, unclear loan programs, approvals that do not match the price, and lenders who will not answer questions.

  • A letter with no lender contact name or license number
  • Vague answers about whether documents were reviewed
  • A buyer who says they need to sell, while the letter assumes they do not
  • A lender who is hard to reach before the contract is even signed

Why does pre-approval quality matter more for new construction?

New-construction timelines are longer than resale, so a weak approval has more time to fall apart before closing.

New-construction timelines are longer than resale. A buyer's situation can change between contract and completion, so a well-documented approval up front, and a lender who keeps the file current, protects the home you are holding for them.
