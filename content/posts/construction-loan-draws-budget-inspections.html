TL;DR

Construction loans pay the builder in stages, called draws, after an inspection confirms each stage of work. Lien waivers, a realistic budget and a contingency protect the buyer and keep the build moving.

On a construction loan, the lender does not hand the builder the full loan amount up front. Funds are released in stages, called draws, as work is completed. Before each draw, an inspector confirms the work was done, and lien waivers or title updates confirm that subcontractors and suppliers are being paid. A clear budget, a realistic draw schedule and a contingency for surprises keep the build moving and protect you.

Key takeaways

  • Construction funds are released in draws tied to completed work.
  • An inspection is typically required before each draw is released.
  • Lien waivers and title updates protect you from unpaid subcontractor claims.
  • A contingency in the budget covers overruns without stopping the project.
  • Changes after closing can affect the budget, the appraisal and the draw schedule.

What is a draw schedule?

A draw schedule lists the stages of construction and how much of the loan is released to the builder when each stage is complete.

The draw schedule is part of your loan and construction contract. It lists the stages of construction, such as foundation, framing, dry-in, mechanicals, drywall and completion, and how much will be released at each. The builder requests a draw when a stage is finished.

What happens at each draw?

At each draw, the builder requests funds, an inspector confirms the work, lien waivers or title updates are checked, and the lender releases the money.

  1. The builder requests a draw for completed work.
  2. An inspector visits the site and confirms the work is done.
  3. Lien waivers or title updates confirm suppliers and subcontractors are being paid.
  4. The lender releases funds to the builder, typically through the title company or directly.

How do you keep the build on budget?

Finalize plans before closing, include a contingency, understand change-order pricing and keep extra cash in reserve for upgrades.

  • Finalize plans and selections before closing, not during construction
  • Include a contingency in the budget for overruns and price changes
  • Ask your builder how change orders are priced and approved
  • Keep extra cash in reserve for upgrades you may want later
  • Respond quickly when the lender or title company asks you to sign off on a draw

Why does Texas construction need extra care?

Texas gives contractors and suppliers lien rights and has specific rules for homestead construction contracts, so documentation at each draw protects you from paying twice.

Texas law gives contractors and suppliers lien rights, and has specific requirements for construction contracts on homesteads. That is why lenders and title companies are careful about lien waivers and documentation at each draw. It protects you from paying twice for the same work.
