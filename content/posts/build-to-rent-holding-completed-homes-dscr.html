When a builder decides to keep completed homes as rentals instead of selling them, the financing question changes from the buyer's income to the property's income. A DSCR (debt-service coverage ratio) loan qualifies primarily on whether the home's rent covers its mortgage payment, taxes, insurance and HOA dues, rather than on the builder's personal tax returns. That can make holding completed inventory a realistic option.

Key takeaways

  • DSCR loans focus on the rental property's income compared with its payment.
  • They can suit builders whose tax returns show reduced income from depreciation and write-offs.
  • Rent is documented by a lease or an appraiser's market-rent estimate.
  • Texas property taxes and insurance affect the ratio, so plan for them.
  • Some programs allow closing in an LLC.

Sell or hold: the decision

Selling returns capital for the next project. Holding builds long-term income and equity, but ties up capital. Many builders do both, selling most homes and holding a few. The financing has to fit whichever path each home takes.

How a DSCR loan looks at a completed home

  • Rent: from a signed lease or an appraiser's market-rent estimate
  • Payment: principal, interest, taxes, insurance and any HOA dues
  • The ratio: rent divided by the payment; stronger ratios generally mean more options
  • Equity and reserves: how much is invested and how much cash remains
  • Ownership: whether the home will be held personally or in an entity

Planning ahead

The best time to plan a hold is before the home is finished: estimate realistic rents, check the tax rate, including any MUD or PID, and confirm how the loan will treat the property once it is complete.
