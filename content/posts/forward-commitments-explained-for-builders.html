A forward commitment is an agreement in which a builder pays a fee to reserve a pool of mortgage money at a set interest rate for a period of time. Buyers who qualify and close within that window can use the committed rate. For builders with homes completing on a predictable schedule, it can lower payments for many buyers at once, often for less than buying down each loan individually.

Key takeaways

  • The builder pays a fee to reserve a pool of loans at a set rate for a set period.
  • Eligible buyers who qualify and close in the window receive the committed rate.
  • It works best with a steady pipeline of homes completing on schedule.
  • Unused portions of the commitment may be lost, so size the pool carefully.
  • Costs may count toward each buyer's seller-concession limit, and rate advertising triggers disclosure rules.

How does a forward commitment work?

  1. Estimate volume. How many homes will close in the window, and at what loan amounts?
  2. Set the terms. The rate, the window and the eligible loan programs are agreed with the lender.
  3. Pay the fee. The builder pays to reserve the pool.
  4. Sell and close. Buyers who qualify and close in the window use the committed rate.
  5. Track usage. The lender and builder track how much of the pool has been used.

What are the risks?

The main risk is paying for capacity you do not use. If construction runs late or sales slow, homes may miss the window. Ask what happens to unused amounts, whether the commitment can be extended and at what cost, and which buyers and programs are eligible.

How does it compare with a buydown?

A buydown is purchased for one buyer's loan. A forward commitment reserves a rate for many future loans. Buydowns are flexible and paid only when a home sells; forward commitments can deliver a lower rate per dollar spent, but they require predicting volume. See forward commitment vs. buydown vs. price cut.

Compliance to plan for

  • Builder contributions may count toward seller-concession limits by loan type
  • Advertising a rate or payment triggers Truth in Lending disclosure requirements
  • Buyers must remain free to choose their lender, and arrangements must comply with RESPA
