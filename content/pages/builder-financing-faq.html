Joseph's Buyer Pre-Approval Checklist walks through each item.[/jb_q][jb_q q="Can a builder require buyers to use a preferred lender?"]No. Buyers are always free to choose their lender. Builders can offer a preferred lender and incentives within program limits, and arrangements must comply with RESPA.[/jb_q][jb_q q="How should we handle buyers who need to sell their current home?"]Find out at the first meeting. If the buyer qualifies carrying both payments, they can sign without a sale contingency. Otherwise options include using existing equity, timing the sale to the build or a realistic contingency.[/jb_q][jb_q q="What if a buyer's situation changes during construction?"]New debt, a job change or large deposits can affect approval. Coach buyers to call their lender before making changes, and ask the lender to update the file before completion.[/jb_q][/jb_faq] [jb_faq title="Incentives and Concessions" eyebrow="Builder FAQ"][jb_q q="Is a rate buydown better than a price cut?"]Often for payment-sensitive buyers, because the same dollars usually lower the monthly payment more, and a buydown protects your comparable sales. A price cut can be better when the buyer needs a lower loan amount. See price cut or rate buydown.[/jb_q][jb_q q="How much can a builder contribute toward a buyer's costs?"]It depends on the loan program, the buyer's down payment and occupancy. Conventional limits range from 3% to 9% for a primary residence, FHA generally allows up to 6%, and VA limits certain concessions to 4%. See seller-concession limits by loan type.[/jb_q][jb_q q="Can builder incentives be paid to the buyer as cash?"]Generally no. Contributions are applied to allowable costs such as closing costs, prepaid items and rate buydowns.[/jb_q][jb_q q="Can we advertise a rate or monthly payment on our homes?"]You can promote financing incentives, but quoting a specific rate or payment triggers federal disclosure requirements. Have your lender review rate-based advertising before it runs.[/jb_q][/jb_faq] [jb_faq title="Construction Timelines and Closing" eyebrow="Builder FAQ"][jb_q q="When should a buyer lock their rate on new construction?"]It depends on the build schedule and the program. Extended locks are available on many programs, usually at extra cost, or the buyer can lock closer to completion. Joseph explains the trade-offs to each buyer.[/jb_q][jb_q q="Can a buyer close before the home is complete?"]Generally no. Lenders typically require the home to be complete, with final inspection or occupancy approval, before a standard purchase loan closes.[/jb_q][jb_q q="Why do new-construction closings get delayed?"]The usual causes are expired documents, rate locks that run out, late completion paperwork, appraisal questions and buyer changes during the build. Appraisals can be done from plans with a completion inspection later.[/jb_q][jb_q q="Can builders finance completed homes they keep as rentals?"]Often, with DSCR loans that qualify on the home's rent rather than personal tax returns.
