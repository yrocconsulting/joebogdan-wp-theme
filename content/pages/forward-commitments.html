How It Works

Reserve the Rate Once, Use It for Many Buyers.

1. Size the pool

Estimate how many homes will close in the window and the total loan volume you want to cover.

2. Pay the commitment fee

The builder pays a fee to reserve that pool of money at an agreed rate for a set period.

3. Buyers qualify and close

Eligible buyers who qualify and close within the window receive the committed rate.

4. Track usage

Joseph tracks which buyers are using the commitment so your team knows what remains and when it expires.

When It Fits

Is a Forward Commitment Right for Your Communities?

Forward commitments work best when you have homes completing on a predictable schedule and buyers who care most about the monthly payment. They are less useful when completion dates are uncertain or when only one or two homes need help.

  • Good fit: a steady pipeline of homes completing in the same window
  • Good fit: payment-sensitive buyers comparing new homes with resale
  • Plan carefully: long or uncertain build schedules that may miss the window
  • Compare first: for a single home, a buyer-specific buydown may cost less

Know the Trade-Offs

What Builders Should Ask Before Committing

  • What happens to the fee if fewer buyers use the commitment than planned?
  • Which loan programs and occupancy types are eligible?
  • How is the cost counted against each buyer's seller-concession limit?
  • Can the commitment be extended if construction runs late, and at what cost?
  • How will rate-based advertising be reviewed for Truth in Lending compliance?

Buyers who qualify and close within that period can use the committed rate.[/jb_q][jb_q q="Is a forward commitment the same as a rate buydown?"]No. A buydown is paid for one buyer's loan. A forward commitment reserves a rate for a group of future loans. Both use builder dollars to lower buyers' rates, but they differ in cost, risk and flexibility.[/jb_q][jb_q q="Do buyers have to use the builder's lender to get the committed rate?"]The committed rate is available through the lender that issued the commitment. Buyers remain free to choose any lender, and arrangements should be reviewed for RESPA compliance.[/jb_q][jb_q q="Can we advertise the committed rate?"]You can, but advertising a rate or payment triggers federal Truth in Lending disclosure requirements. Have rate-based advertising reviewed before it runs.[/jb_q][/jb_faq]

Forward commitment availability, terms and costs depend on market conditions and program guidelines and may change. Buyers are subject to credit approval. Builders and lenders must comply with RESPA; buyers are always free to choose their own lender.
