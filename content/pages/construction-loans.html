Before You Break Ground

The Questions Every Custom-Home Buyer Asks.

“Can I finance the land and the build together?”

Often, yes. A construction-to-permanent loan can cover buying the lot, building the home and the long-term mortgage in a single loan.

“What if I already own my lot?”

Equity in land you own can often count toward your down payment, which can reduce the cash you need at closing.

“Do I have to qualify twice?”

Not with a one-time close. You qualify and close once, before construction starts, instead of re-qualifying when the home is finished.

“How does my builder get paid?”

Funds are released in stages, called draws, as construction milestones are inspected and approved.

“What happens if the build runs over?”

Budgets include a contingency for a reason. Joseph helps you plan for overruns and delays before they happen, not after.

“Can I use any builder?”

Your builder has to be reviewed and approved by the lender. Joseph starts that review early so it never holds up your closing.

How It Works

One Loan From Lot to Keys.

A one-time-close loan (also called construction-to-permanent) combines short-term construction financing and your long-term mortgage. You sign once. During construction, the loan pays your builder in draws. When the home is complete, the loan becomes your regular mortgage without a second closing.

Choosing a Path

One-Time Close or Two-Time Close?

A one-time close means one approval, one set of closing costs and no re-qualifying at the end. A two-time close uses a separate short-term construction loan and then a new mortgage when the home is finished: two closings and two approvals, but more flexibility to shop the permanent loan. The right choice depends on your timeline, your cash and how stable your income will be through the build.
